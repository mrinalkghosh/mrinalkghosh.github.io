  1. 2026Preprint

    Hardness of Approximation for Polynomial Optimization over Sphere for Linear Programming Relaxation Hierarchy

    Mrinalkanti Ghosh

    Synopsis of the moment LP paper

    Let P be a random spherical harmonic of even degree d ≥ 2 in n variables. Fix C > 0 and a positive multiple q of 2d with q ≤ n. I prove a degree-q moment LP integrality gap of Ωd,C((n/q)d/2 − 1/2 / √(q log n)) for maximizing |P| on the unit sphere. For any family of at most nCq valid polynomial tests of degree at most q chosen independently of P, the gap holds with high probability over P. The LP bound is the relaxed optimum for Pq/d raised to the power d/q.

  2. 2020FOCS

    Sum-of-Squares Lower Bounds for Sherrington-Kirkpatrick via Planted Affine Planes

    Mrinalkanti Ghosh, Fernando Granha Jeronimo, Chris Jones, Aaron Potechin, and Goutham Rajendran

    Synopsis of the Sherrington–Kirkpatrick paper

    How well can sum of squares certify the maximum energy of a random Sherrington–Kirkpatrick instance on n spins? We show that, with high probability, even degree nδ for some δ > 0 fails to improve the spectral upper bound by a constant factor. The proof starts by asking whether one can certify that random points do not all lie on two parallel hyperplanes equidistant from the origin.

  3. 2019SODA

    Approximability of p → q Matrix Norms: Generalized Krivine Rounding and Hypercontractive Hardness

    Vijay Bhattiprolu, Mrinalkanti Ghosh, Venkatesan Guruswami, Euiwoong Lee, and Madhur Tulsiani

    Synopsis of the SODA paper on matrix norms

    We generalize Krivine’s rounding to approximate the norm of a matrix as a map from ℓp to ℓq. The approximation factor is independent of dimension when p ≥ 2 ≥ q. For 1 < p < q < 2 or 2 < p < q < ∞, we rule out even a polynomial-time constant-factor approximation unless NP has randomized quasipolynomial-time algorithms.

  4. 2017FOCS

    Weak Decoupling, Polynomial Folds, and Approximate Optimization over the Sphere

    Vijay Bhattiprolu, Mrinalkanti Ghosh, Venkatesan Guruswami, Euiwoong Lee, and Madhur Tulsiani

    Synopsis of the weak decoupling paper

    Let P be a homogeneous degree-d polynomial in n variables, and let q be a positive multiple of 2d with q ≤ n. We approximate the maximum of |P| on the unit sphere within a factor of Od((n/q)d/2 − 1) for arbitrary real coefficients and Od((n/q)d/4 − 1/2) for nonnegative coefficients. Weak decoupling and polynomial folds give a deterministic nO(q)-time algorithm with these guarantees relative to the corresponding degree-q relaxations.

  5. 2017CCC

    From Weak to Strong LP Gaps for All CSPs

    Mrinalkanti Ghosh and Madhur Tulsiani

    Synopsis of the CSP integrality gaps paper

    Starting from an integrality gap for the basic LP of a constraint satisfaction problem, we construct instances on n variables that retain the gap, up to an arbitrarily small additive loss, through Ω(log n / log log n) rounds of Sherali–Adams. Together with earlier results on extended formulations, this shows that the basic LP captures the worst-case approximation power of polynomial-size LPs for CSPs.

  6. 2014CCR

    Ornstein Isomorphism and Algorithmic Randomness

    Mrinalkanti Ghosh, Satyadev Nandakumar, and Atanu Pal

    Synopsis of the Ornstein isomorphism paper

    An isomorphism in ergodic theory may discard a set of measure zero. For two computable mixing Markov shifts with equal entropy, we adapt the finitary coding of Keane and Smorodinsky to give an isomorphism defined on every Martin-Löf random sequence. The map is layerwise computable, so a bound on the sequence’s randomness deficiency suffices to compute its image from the sequence.

  7. 2012ALT

    Predictive Complexity and Generalized Entropy Rate of Stationary Ergodic Processes

    Mrinalkanti Ghosh and Satyadev Nandakumar

    Synopsis of the predictive complexity paper

    For a stationary ergodic binary source, Shannon entropy rate gives the long-run loss per symbol under optimal prediction with logarithmic loss. We extend this relation to other losses, including square loss. For a strongly regular mixable game with predictive complexity, the predictive complexity per symbol converges almost surely to the source’s generalized entropy rate for that game.
